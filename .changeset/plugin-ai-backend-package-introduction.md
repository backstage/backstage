'@backstage/plugin-ai-backend': minor
---

Added a new `@backstage/plugin-ai-backend` package: a backend plugin that exposes the managed AI service over HTTP. Authenticated users create their own AI sessions and conversations, submit messages that stream partial and final answers as server-sent events, and read their conversation transcripts and per-session audit trails. Every route except readiness runs under the caller's user credentials and requires the `ai.session.use` permission, model and tool dispatch are authorized per attempt against the fixed `ai.model.use` and `ai.tool.use` permissions, whose granularity comes from the model and tool rules the plugin registers, and audit reads additionally require `ai.audit.read`; one user can never read or drive another user's session. The plugin's `/alpha` entry re-exports the model, tool, and feature permission rules, their conditions, and the conditional-decision helpers from `@backstage/ai-node/alpha`, so policy authors already depending on the plugin can import them from either package. An unauthenticated `GET /health` readiness endpoint reports the service's fail-open health evaluation — derived from its `ai.observability.*` events — as `200` with `status: "ok"` or `503` with `status: "degraded"`, carrying identifiers, counts, and durations only. Every event a message submission's inline SSE stream carries — partial snapshots, the answer, settlement — is also published through the Backstage signals service on the `ai.session.<sessionId>.<conversationId>` channel, so the session owner's other connections (another tab, a frontend component signed in as the owner) can follow a submission they did not place; the inline stream to the submitter is unchanged. Signals delivery is gated publish-side — each signal is addressed to the session owner alone, and this signals version has no channel-level authorization, so a different user subscribed to the channel receives nothing — and it is live-only with no replay: durable outcome observation remains the audit journal. A submission that settles after its SSE client is gone sends the owner a notification through the Backstage notifications service instead of disappearing silently; a still-streaming client receives settlement inline with no duplicate notification.
