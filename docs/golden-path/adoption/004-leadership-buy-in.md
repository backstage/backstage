Leadership investment should follow evidence, not precede it. Bring the PoC
results to leaders when the adoption group can explain the problem, what the
experiment changed, what remains uncertain, and why continued work may be
valuable.

**Decision prompt:** What continued commitment is justified by the evidence?

## Make the case from PoC evidence

Start with the outcome rather than the portal. Explain:

- who experiences the problem and how often;
- the current cost in time, handoffs, support work, or risk;
- what the PoC changed compared with the baseline;
- what the PoC did not test;
- what the next stage is expected to teach the organization.

Daily active users may show reach, but they do not prove that a portal reduced
onboarding time or removed toil. Use measures that match the original problem,
supported by direct feedback from participants.

## Describe the commitment

Backstage is a framework used to build an internal product. Continued investment
may require:

- an executive sponsor who connects the work to wider priorities and removes
  cross-team blockers;
- a product team with capacity to research, build, operate, and lead adoption;
- a technical partner for the shared environment and its integrations;
- time from participating developers and teams that own source systems;
- communication, support, security, and governance partners as the audience
  expands.

Do not present these as universal staffing numbers. Show which responsibilities
exist, who can own them now, and which commitments remain uncertain.

## Agree on the next investment

Ask leadership to align on:

1. **The outcome:** The behavior or result continued work should improve.
2. **The sponsor:** The leader accountable for organizational support.
3. **The product team:** The team accountable for the Backstage experience.
4. **The pilot:** The users, environment, and workflow the next stage will test.
5. **The evidence:** The measures and feedback that will guide another decision.
6. **The cost and risks:** The known demands, uncertainties, and accepted
   tradeoffs.
7. **The review date:** When leaders and the product team will revisit the
   investment.

Leadership may approve a narrow pilot, request an adjusted approach, or pause
continued work. Record the reasoning so that the next review starts from the
same evidence.

## Worked example

The platform PM presents the ownership-discovery PoC. Eight of ten developers
completed the task in under five minutes, and the median time fell from 15
minutes to three. The remaining failures came from missing source documentation.

Leadership funds a product team to evolve the PoC into a shared pilot for 50
on-call engineers. A VP of Engineering sponsors the work. The pilot will test
single sign-on, automated ownership ingestion, support demand, and whether users
return without a guided session. The investment will be reviewed after six
weeks.

## Before you continue

You should now have an explicit investment decision that names the sponsor,
product team, technical partner, participating teams, and the evidence the
shared pilot must produce. Keep the commitment time-bound so its costs, risks,
and remaining uncertainty can be reviewed.

## Learn from adopters

### [Backstage in the board room](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kb3F98fKvaQ)

_BackstageCon North America, 2024 · 32:01 video_

- Frame the portal in terms of strategic alignment and value delivery, not only
  platform-team efficiency.
- Bring executive sponsors questions that the catalog can help answer, then
  connect continued funding to those outcomes.

### [The journey of deploying Backstage in a large organization](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l7OQdrUkoVI)

_KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America, 2025 · 31:32 video_

- Follow an adopter from initial sponsorship and financing through
  organization-wide adoption.
- Position the portal as a response to developer cognitive load, then give the
  product team time to change how developers deliver and manage software.
